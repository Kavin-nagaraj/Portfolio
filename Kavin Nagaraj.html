Policy, Control & Evidence

The core of the platform. Teams turn framework requirements into controls, then link the policies and evidence that prove each control works.

My role
Research, flows, wireframes, UI, prototypes and the design system
Users
Compliance admins, policy owners, approvers
Frameworks
ISO 27001, SOC 2, PCI DSS, CIS
Timeline
2025 · Cytrusst
Tools
Figma, FigJam
Platform
Desktop web app

All names, numbers and screens use sample data.

Overview

The challenge

Every control depends on policies and evidence owned by different people, each with approvals, due dates and reminders. Without one place to see them, it is hard to know what is blocked and why.

The goal

One connected register where a control, its policies and its evidence move through the same clear lifecycle, from first upload to approval.

What I designed

  • Registers for controls, policies and evidence with bulk assign and bulk edit
  • Two-step create and duplicate flows with one shared framework picker
  • The upload-to-approval lifecycle with reminders, escalation and rework
  • AI policy templates and clause suggestions that list the gaps
  • KRI dashboards, plus admin and normal-user views

Research

What I learned

How I learned it

  • Walkthroughs with compliance analysts
  • Review of framework clauses and policy templates
  • Mapping who creates, reviews and approves
  • Review of common GRC tools
01

Status is the real task list

People open the register to answer one question: what is waiting on me? Status had to lead every row.

02

Links matter more than records

A control is only useful with its policies and evidence attached, so linking had to work from both sides.

03

Blank pages slow people down

Owners spend the most time on a first draft, which pointed to AI policy templates.

  • Make linking and bulk actions fast in large registers
  • Help owners start from a solid draft

  • Items waiting on approval past their due date
  • Policies started from an AI template

Design decisions

What shaped the module

Links work both ways

A policy lists its controls, and a control lists its policies and evidence. People can start from either side.

Color plus a label

Every status has a color and a word, so blocked items stand out in long lists without relying on color alone.

Bulk actions for scale

Teams manage hundreds of records, so assign, reminders, escalation and risk linking all work in bulk.

Locked actions explain why

Normal users see disabled actions with a tooltip that says why, instead of buttons that silently disappear.

What changed after testing

What I changed

What we saw

What I changed

Comments sit next to the evidence, with a count on the tab.

What we saw

What I changed

Learnings

What I took away

  1. 01Design the status system first. Every screen leans on it.
  2. 02For compliance teams, bulk actions are a core feature, not an extra.
  3. 03AI drafts work best when they land inside the normal approval flow.

Assessment Audit with AI

Auditors check each requirement with an AI summary, a confidence score and suggested findings, while the final call stays with them.

My role
Flows, UI, AI states and prototypes
Users
Internal and external auditors, auditees
Frameworks
ISO 27001, SOC 2
Timeline
2025 · Cytrusst
Tools
Figma, FigJam
Platform
Desktop web app

Overview

The challenge

An audit means checking every requirement against its policies and evidence, then writing findings. Done by hand, it is slow, and results vary from one auditor to the next.

The goal

Let AI do the first pass on every requirement and show its reasoning, so auditors spend their time on judgment instead of paperwork.

What I designed

  • An AI dashboard with requirements met and confidence per requirement
  • Requirement detail with an AI verdict, strengths, gaps and reasoning
  • Document checks that show the score before and after an upload
  • AI-detected findings with impact, root cause and mitigation steps
  • A four-step Add Finding flow and an audit chatbot

  • Findings that follow one structure

Frustrations

  • Copying the same text into every finding

  • See how an upload changes the result

Frustrations

  • Finding out about gaps too late
AL

Plans audits, reviews findings and signs off reports.

Needs

  • See progress across all requirements
  • Trust the reasoning behind each verdict

Frustrations

  • No quick way to compare frameworks

Research

What I learned

How I learned it

  • Sessions with internal auditors
  • Review of audit checklists and report formats
  • Mapping requirement, policy and evidence links
  • Review of AI output with auditors
01

Auditors trust reasons, not scores

A confidence number on its own was ignored. Showing the justification and evidence next to it made people use it.

02

Missing documents cause most delays

Most of the time goes into chasing files, so the AI card names the document to upload and the score it would reach.

03

Findings need one structure

Impact, root cause and mitigation steps in the same order made findings faster to write and review.

Goals

What good looks like

Design goals

  • Give auditors a fast first pass on every requirement
  • Keep the auditor's judgment as the final call
  • Turn gaps into well-structured findings quickly

What we track

  • Time to review one requirement
  • Requirements with missing evidence
  • AI pre-filled findings that needed edits

Designing the Risk Register for SBI

Overview

The challenge

Risk registers grow slowly because every risk is written and scored by hand. AI can draft them, but people only trust a score when they can see where it came from.

The goal

Make every AI number explainable, and let analysts turn a suggestion into a real risk in one step.

What I designed

  • AI risk cards that expand into reasoning, mitigations and a matched control
  • Risk groups with AI assessment questions and a review step
  • A four-step manual flow with a reviewer and up to five approval levels
  • Mitigation tracking with AI-scored steps and additional controls
  • A contextual risk search and a chat assistant inside the register

Overview

The challenge

Assessing one vendor touches questionnaires, contracts, approvals, external scans and follow-up actions. Each part has its own owners and deadlines.

The goal

One vendor record where every check, grade and deadline is visible, from onboarding to renewal.

Overview
